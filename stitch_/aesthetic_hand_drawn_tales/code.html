The Alchemy of
Painted Light

In the heart of traditional animation lies a secret medium: gouache. Unlike the transparency of watercolor, gouache offers a heavy, opaque dreamscape—a world where light is built layer by layer, stroke by stroke.

The "Ma" of Composition

"Ma" is the Japanese concept of negative space, or the "gap." In our studio, we treat the empty canvas not as a void, but as a breath. It is the silence between notes that makes the music.

In background painting, this manifests as vast skies and quiet corners where the eye can rest, allowing the narrative to breathe through the environment itself.

The Gouache Technique

Traditional animation backgrounds are predominantly painted with Nicker Gouache, a high-pigment professional paint from Japan. It is prized for its ability to produce rich, saturated colors that remain matte when dry.

Artists begin with a light pencil sketch, often on large sheets of paper. The process involves 'blocking in' the largest shapes first—the sky, the mountains, the ground—before layering finer details. Because gouache can be reworked even after drying, artists can blend colors directly on the paper to create soft atmospheric gradients.
